APM Competence Assessment

The APM Competence Framework (3rd edition) defines 29 competences across life cycles, governance, people, planning and delivery. Each competence is expressed in terms of knowledge and application, with a five‑point rating scale from Unaware (0) to Expert (5).

Life cycles

The framework describes life cycles as the structure underpinning deployment, covering linear, iterative and hybrid approaches. It emphasises selecting an appropriate life cycle based on outputs, outcomes, benefits, uncertainty, novelty and organisational risk appetite.

  • Knowledge of available life cycle options and organisational culture.
  • Ability to analyse, select and implement suitable life cycles.
  • Continuous improvement through feedback, data and refinement.

Governance arrangements

Governance arrangements define authority, accountability and control over change initiatives. They ensure alignment with organisational governance and provide clear reporting and decision‑making structures.

  • Designing governance structures appropriate to context and complexity.
  • Clarifying ownership, roles, escalation routes and reporting.
  • Maintaining confidence through robust data and adherence to standards.

Sustainability

Sustainability is defined as meeting current stakeholder needs without compromising future generations, embedding environmental, social, economic and administrative considerations across the life cycle.

  • Aligning change initiatives with organisational sustainability agendas.
  • Assessing impacts, metrics and stakeholder expectations.
  • Embedding sustainable behaviours, monitoring and continuous improvement.

Financial management

Financial management ensures affordability, secures funding and controls expenditure in line with organisational financial systems, optimising the business case.

  • Consistent estimating and investment appraisal.
  • Capital and revenue planning aligned to strategy.
  • Financial reporting, control limits and variance management.

Business case

Business cases justify initiation and continuation of change initiatives, and must be revisited at key review points as circumstances evolve.

  • Identifying value, benefits and strategic alignment.
  • Analysing options, risks and commercial approaches.
  • Maintaining and refining the case through change control.

Portfolio shaping

Portfolio shaping groups and prioritises change initiatives to deliver strategic objectives, balancing business‑as‑usual with change and optimising return on investment.

  • Translating strategic objectives into required change.
  • Balancing capacity, risk and value across initiatives.
  • Ensuring coherent, strategically aligned portfolios.
